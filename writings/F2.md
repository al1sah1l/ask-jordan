# F2: Problem Framing

## Domain

Interest exploration for middle schoolers. Around the world, middle schoolers (students ages 11 to 14 years old) begin to explore potential future paths for their life. Most students are bounded by the experiences they already have had and the experiences of those around them, especially their family members. They also might have preconceived notions about certain interests and jobs that could deter them without allowing the student to fully explore the option. Additionally, students ages 11 to 14 begin to develop their own hobbies and interests. Both of these developments are motivated by the growth of individuals into preteens and teenagers who have more autonomy and personal identity. The primary stakeholders in this domain are middle school students looking to explore potential interests. Teachers are also a stakeholder in this domain, as they likely want to help students explore their interests and find their passions. Parents are another stakeholder in this domain. Parents want to help their children find their passions and explore the opportunities that the world has for them.

## Bad situations

- **Limited exposure to new interests.**
  - A middle schooler named George wants to discover what he enjoys but doesn't know where to start. He tends to stick to familiar activities or follow his peers, this limits his opportunities to discover new interests. For example, George might have a natural talent for volleyball but never considers trying it simply because all his classmates play soccer.
- **Classroom pace doesn't fit every student.**
  - Students learn at different speeds, but a standardized classroom pace can make it difficult for them to recognize their strengths. A student who struggles to keep up may conclude they are "not academically gifted," while a student who is ready to move ahead may become disengaged. For example, Regina takes longer than her classmates to memorize maths formulas. This makes her believe that she isn't talented at STEM. However, her ability to grasp complex concepts quickly and develop a deep understanding of them goes unnoticed because the classroom emphasizes memorization over conceptual understanding.
- **Social pressure narrows choices.**
  - Middle schoolers may choose activities based on what their peers consider "cool" rather than their genuine interests or abilities. Social expectations and stereotypes can discourage them from exploring unfamiliar activities and discovering hidden talents. For example, George's classmates believe that "cool guys play football" and that figure skating is "for girls." As a result, George never considers trying figure skating, even though his natural agility and quickness might make him particularly good at it. Without exposure to the sport or relatable role models, he may never discover this potential interest.

## Evidence

### Student Interest
According to a 2022 study, 85% of middle schoolers in the United States are already thinking about future careers and 87% are interested in ways to match their skills and interests to potential careers ([Edutopia](https://www.edutopia.org/article/8-ways-to-help-middle-schoolers-imagine-their-future-careers)).

### Student Behaviors
The Association for Career and Technical Education (2018) identified middle school as a core period for career awareness, career knowledge accumulation, and the preliminary establishment of career goals ([frontiers](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/391568173_Improving_career_readiness_in_middle_school_students_a_systematic_review_of_intervention_approaches/link/682eb4768a76251f22e526b6/download)). Despite middle school being shown as a core period for career development, studies show that most middle school students have unclear career perceptions, misjudge the skill demands of jobs, and experience a disconnect between education and career. Students begin to rule out unfamiliar career opportunities as early as middle school, meaning that providing students with opportunities to explore different options than what surrounds them in middle school can prevent students from ruling out options early ([ECS](https://www.ecs.org/state-of-middle-school-career-exploration/)). Earlier career exposure can increase students' likelihood of successfully transitioning into postsecondary options that are increasingly necessary for good jobs.

### Challenges to Career Exploration
Some of the main challenges that students face in career and interest exploration in middle school are limited funding, pressure on core academic time, and overburdened school counselors. These challenges limit the abilities of students to explore new opportunities and feel supported throughout the process.
